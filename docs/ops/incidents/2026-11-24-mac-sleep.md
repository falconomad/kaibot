# Incident: the Mac slept for 17 hours, 24-25 Nov 2026 (audit 2 ticket 5.5, #685)

**Status:** closed for the session, root cause **unknown**. **Impact:** no lost money or unmanaged risk on record;
the engine missed the 25 Nov open by 78 minutes and the multi-day decision of 25 Nov came late (11:05 ET), and
was sent and filled in that afternoon's window. Written blameless, from Product's reading of the production database
on 25 Nov. Times are UTC, with New York time (ET) in brackets.

## What went right

- **The late-decision path worked.** The old engine would have lost the 25 Nov decision; this one made it at 11:05
  ET once the store had filled, and it went out in the same afternoon's window.
- **Positions were never unprotected by design.** Multi-day sleeves carry broker-held GTC stops whatever the Mac does
  ([failsafe.md](../failsafe.md)). The watchdog, once awake, cancelled entries, checked stops and kept the multi-day
  positions, as it should.

## What went wrong

- **The Mac slept although the engine was held awake.** The engine process was already wrapped in `caffeinate -is`
  at `24064ed` (`scripts/run_engine.sh` line 60: `awake() { ... caffeinate -is "$@" ... }`); the service wrapper in
  `scripts/service_run.sh` added `caffeinate -i -w` on its own pid. The Mac slept anyway.
- **Nothing on the Mac could tell anyone.** The watchdog and the Healthchecks pinger run on the sleeping Mac. The
  watchdog's halt was written 17 hours after the last beat.
- **The margin was luck.** Had the store still been incomplete after that session's send window, the
  one-session-back rule would have lost the rebalance (second engine audit, "The 24-25 Nov sleep").
- **A restart inside market hours.** The owner's 19:41 UTC (14:41 ET) restart onto `246f01a` fell inside
  09:30-16:00 ET, the hours `CLAUDE.md` keeps free of engine deploys. It went well; it is noted so that the next one
  is planned.

## Root cause: unknown

It is **not established** why the Mac slept. The engine was already held by `caffeinate -is` (prevent idle sleep
and, on AC power, system sleep), which should have kept a Mac mini awake while the engine ran. What we know:

- `caffeinate` assertions cover only while their process runs, and they never stop a sleep chosen from the Apple
  menu, a lid close, a forced sleep (`pmset sleepnow`) or a thermal or low-power sleep.
- The `pmset` settings on the Mac before the incident were not recorded, so we cannot tell whether a scheduled or
  idle setting won over the assertion. The macOS power log (`pmset -g log`) for 24-25 Nov was not read before this
  document was written; it is the one record that would name the sleep reason (for example "Sleep: Using AC
  (Charge:...) ... Software Sleep pid=..." or "Idle Sleep").
- Nothing recorded the engine going down that night: no exit, no crash, no halt. Yet a new process started at
  15:48:06, 11 s after the watchdog woke. Whether the old process was still running through the sleep (and exited on
  wake, for example after a lease or heartbeat check) or had stopped earlier, letting the Mac sleep idle, is not
  known. The engine and service logs of that night (`logs/`, `service.sh logs`) were not read for this document.

## Remediations

| Done or started | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Done, 25 Nov (owner) | `sudo pmset -c sleep 0 disksleep 0` on the Mac: no system or disk sleep on mains power, whatever runs | the Mac; recorded in [always_on.md](../always_on.md) |
| Done, #680 | Keep-awake guard: the service holds `caffeinate -i -s -w <run_engine.sh pid>` for each engine run; `--check` reports the `pmset` settings; an `engine_gap` event and a Healthchecks fail ping when the engine was off for more than an hour | `scripts/service_run.sh`, `scripts/run_engine.sh`, `src/kaibot/engine/gap.py` |
| In progress, #692 | The engine fetches its own daily bars, so a missed night job no longer leaves the store partial at the open | `src/kaibot/engine/` (5.12) |

## Open questions (not on record yet)

1. **Did the Supabase dead-man act between 14:30 and 15:48 UTC?** With the heartbeat stale since 22:45 UTC, it
   should have read the account from the first minute after the open and written one `halt` row of kind `deadman`
   ("engine silent ..."). Product's reading lists only the watchdog's halt. Read-only check:

   ```sql
   select ts, kind, reason from kaibot.halt
    where ts between '2026-11-25 13:00+00' and '2026-11-25 16:00+00' order by ts;
   select id, ts, state, result from kaibot.deadman_pass
    where ts between '2026-11-25 13:00+00' and '2026-11-25 16:00+00' order by ts;
   ```

   No `deadman_pass` rows at all would mean the dead-man itself did not run (pg_cron, Vault keys), which is its own
   incident (audit 2 ticket 7.7, a dead-man failure alerts nobody).
2. **Did Healthchecks reach the owner?** With no pings, the check should have turned red about 20 minutes after the
   last success ping (about 23:05 UTC on 24 Nov) and e-mailed. The check's log on healthchecks.io answers it.
3. **Why did the Mac sleep?** `pmset -g log | grep -E "Sleep|Wake|Assertions" ` on the Mac, for 24 Nov 22:00 to
   25 Nov 16:00 UTC.

When these are answered, add them here; if the root cause is found, replace "unknown" above.
