# Trade Slower, Prove Everything, Expect Less

Kaibot's dip rule lost because it sat on the wrong side of the trade it was built on. Short-term reversal profits are the wage paid to whoever supplies liquidity; Kaibot bought and sold with marketable orders, so it paid that wage twice per trade, in the most liquid stocks where it is smallest, behind a 0.63% stop that contradicts a mean-reversion bet, and then went flat at 15:55, just before the hours in which both the equity premium and the reversal premium have historically been earned. At one round trip a day per slot of capital, a 6 bp cost eats roughly half a Sharpe-ratio unit a year, more than any single retail rule earns, so −59% against SPY's +209% is arithmetic and not bad luck. Durable systematic traders work the other way round: a few slow, simple, economically motivated rules blended across lookbacks, positions sized by volatility, costs capped at a third of expected edge, backtested Sharpe ratios cut in half, and nothing traded until it has passed tests whose pass marks were written down beforehand. For this account the rules that deserve an honest ten-year test are trend-filtered large-cap momentum held for weeks (the core candidate), the long side of the SPY "noise band" intraday momentum rule with an optional overnight carry, and a few low-prior one-shot tests, including the existing opening-range breakout, which will most likely be deleted. Published intraday headlines of 20% to 42% a year rest on 4x leverage, short selling and commission-only costs; long-only and unlevered they shrink to low single digits or nothing. The engine should stay custom but be rebuilt so that one decision core runs against either Alpaca or a simulated broker that enforces Alpaca's real order rules, because neither NautilusTrader (no Alpaca adapter, mid-rewrite) nor LEAN (paid tooling, a fragile Alpaca path) is worth adopting now. The likely outcome is a production-grade engine whose best portfolio lands within about two percentage points a year of SPY with shallower drawdowns; ten years of data cannot prove an edge that small, so "nothing beats holding SPY" is a probable and legitimate finding.

## Why −59% was the expected result, not bad luck

Kaibot's own figures separate two problems. Before costs, 10,124 out-of-sample trades lost $4,139, about 41 cents a trade: no edge at all. The cost model then removed a further $83,400 or so, about $8.24 a trade, which at 6 bp implies an average position near $13,700 (our arithmetic from the owner's test figures). The published evidence predicts both the missing edge and the fatal toll.

### Reversal profits are a wage for supplying liquidity, and Kaibot was buying it

Nagel's study treats the return of short-term reversal strategies as a stand-in for what liquidity providers earn: the payoff swings over time, climbs with the VIX, jumps in turmoil when liquidity is withdrawn, and averages close to nothing for industry-level portfolios ([Nagel, NBER w17653](https://nber.org/papers/w17653)). Quantpedia files reversal under liquidity provision, likens it to a short-volatility position, and warns that its frequent trading in costly names can make it unexecutable at a profit ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)). Kaibot inverted the role. It bought dips with marketable orders and sold with marketable orders, so every trade crossed the spread twice. Its counterparties are professionals: in the second quarter of 2026 all of Alpaca's held, non-directed equity orders went to four wholesalers, which pay Alpaca **12% of the quoted spread** on marketable orders ([Alpaca 606 report, Q2 2026](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2026Q2.pdf)). The wage Kaibot hoped to collect was being collected from it.

Even without that handicap the premium is thin. In Novy-Marx and Velikov's 1963–2012 sample, one-month reversal earned 0.37% a month before costs (t = 1.71) on 90.9% monthly turnover and **lost 1.28% a month after costs (t = −6.02)**; absent cost mitigation, no strategy turning over more than half its book each month kept a significant net return ([Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). A 2012–2021 study prices short-term reversal's trading cost at 3.4% to 6.8% a year and finds it largely unprofitable net ([Goyal, Jegadeesh and Wu](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/25551_Price_Impact.pdf)). Where net profits do reappear, roughly 30 to 50 bp a week, the recipe is a weekly horizon, only the largest stocks, a long-short book, a turnover-reducing construction and institutional execution ([de Groot, Huij and Zhou](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i2p371-382.html)). None of those conditions describes an intraday, long-only, liquidity-taking rule. Kaibot's failed high-VIX variation fits this reading: volatility raises the wage of those who provide liquidity, which does nothing for an engine that consumes it (our interpretation).

Stock-level intraday evidence is worse still. Lou, Polk and Skouras split 1993–2013 returns into overnight and intraday parts and find that short-term reversal in stocks earned **+0.93% a month overnight and −1.05% a month intraday** ([Lou, Polk and Skouras](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)). Kaibot held only the losing half. Buying gap-downs, the dip rule's nearest published cousin, managed a single positive net window after its 2013 publication in one replication ([Financial Noob](https://financialnoob.substack.com/p/buy-on-gap-strategy-and-its-performance)) and was judged plainly bad in liquid names over 2014–2020 in another ([QuantRocket](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/buy-or-sell-down-gaps/)). The one stock-level intraday reversal with academic support is confined to the last half hour of the session, in a sample ending 2019, and the sources reviewed give neither its size nor its cost sensitivity ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/end-of-trading/)).

### One round trip a day needs a Sharpe ratio no single rule has

Robert Carver's cost rule is the most usable yardstick. Divide the round-trip cost by the instrument's annualised volatility to express it in Sharpe-ratio units, multiply by round trips per year, and never let the annual total exceed one third of a conservatively estimated pre-cost Sharpe ratio; because most single rules sit below 0.4, the practical ceiling is **0.13 Sharpe units a year**, and at retail cost levels the framework rules day trading out ([the7circles on Carver](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24243)). Applied to Kaibot (our arithmetic, assuming 30% annualised volatility for a large-cap stock), a 5 bp round trip is 0.0017 Sharpe units, 250 round trips a year cost 0.42, and the one-third rule then demands a pre-cost Sharpe of about 1.25 from one rule; at the modelled 6 bp the figures are 0.5 and 1.5. Carver's own benchmark ladder puts a simple one-rule mechanical system at about 0.24 and an experienced, diversified retail systematic trader at about 1.0 ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/03/how-much-risk-should-we-take.html)). Run in reverse, the 0.13 budget permits roughly 40 to 130 round trips a year, a holding period of two to six trading days, and only for a rule whose Sharpe of 0.4 is real (our arithmetic). Trade count offered no escape: the fundamental law of active management sets the information ratio equal to forecast skill times the square root of independent bets times a transfer coefficient that shrinks under constraints such as a short-selling ban ([AnalystPrep](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/state-and-interpret-the-fundamental-law-of-active-portfolio-management-including-its-component-terms-transfer-coefficient-information-coefficient-breadth-and-active-risk-aggressiveness/)). Thousands of same-day dip buys in correlated large caps are not thousands of independent bets, and skill near zero multiplied by any breadth is still zero.

### A tight stop on a reversal bet, and an exit before the paid hours

Kaminski and Lo show that a stop-loss lowers expected return when prices follow a random walk, hurts under mean reversion, and helps only when returns trend or a bad regime can be identified; in their 1993–2011 futures tests, stop policies defined over daily windows mostly carried negative premiums while horizons beyond a month could be positive ([Kaminski and Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). A study of Stockholm large caps found the 5% trailing stop was the only level that underperformed buy and hold, and 15% to 20% did best ([Quant Investing](https://www.quant-investing.com/blog/truths-about-stop-losses-that-nobody-wants-to-believe)). Kaibot paired a mean-reversion entry with a stop 0.63% below it, and 45% of trades hit that stop. A rule that bets on a bounce was closing the position at the point where its own premise said the bounce was most valuable. Either the premise or the stop was wrong; the ten-year result says the premise.

The 15:55 exit removed the rule from the hours that pay. A dollar held in SPY only during the trading day grew to $1.20 over thirty years, while a dollar held only overnight grew to **$17.27** ([Elm Wealth](https://elmwealth.com/night-shift/)). Lou, Polk and Skouras put the market premium at 0.55% a month overnight against 0.38% intraday and find that stock momentum is earned entirely overnight ([Lou, Polk and Skouras](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)). A long book that is flat every night competes against SPY's +209% with the least rewarded part of the day.

### The evidence arrived after the engine

The rule was designed on about 17 months of data, and only afterwards met a decade of history. Bailey and co-authors show that with five years of data, 45 independent configurations are enough to produce an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with no skill ([Bailey et al.](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)); the same formula applied to 17 months allows about five (our calculation). Carver's figures say a true Sharpe of 0.5 needs about ten years before its lower confidence band clears zero, and 0.3 needs about 37 ([the7circles on Carver](https://the7circles.uk/?p=29002)). Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, in-sample metrics explained 1% to 2% of out-of-sample performance, and developers who ran more backtests had larger shortfalls ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies)). The ten-year walk-forward was the right test and it gave a clean verdict. What was immature was the order of work: one rule, a short sample, a live engine built before the evidence, and a backtest that ran through a separate simulator re-implementing the trading rules, so that even a good result would have described different code from the code that trades.

## Slow rules earn a test; fast headline numbers shrink to low single digits

Permission to hold for days changes the economics more than any parameter could. A monthly momentum book turning over 34.5% of its holdings a month costs about 25 bp a year at 6 bp per round trip, and about 14 bp with an entry-and-exit band (our arithmetic from the turnover figures in [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). Cost stops being the binding constraint. The question becomes whether any gross advantage over SPY exists in liquid large caps for a long-only book, and the evidence says: a little, unreliably.

### Multi-day: trend-filtered momentum is the best-supported core

Stock momentum, ranked on the past twelve months' return with the latest month skipped, is the best-replicated price anomaly. The long-short factor earned 8.3% a year at a Sharpe of 0.50 over 1927–2013, with the winners contributing 5.5 points of market-adjusted return and the losers 5.1, so about half is available without shorting; large caps earned 6.8% against 9.8% for small caps, and the large-cap Sharpe over 1991–2013 was only 0.24 ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en)). It survives the strictest replication alongside residual and industry momentum ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/)), but the premium halved after publication, from 10.9% a year before 1994 to 5.5% over 1994–2015 ([Swedroe](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-really-dead)), and US funds with momentum in their name delivered a value-add of −2.2% a year as of 2017 ([Research Affiliates via etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)). Live long-only products tell the same modest story. MTUM's ten-year annualised return was 16.50% against 15.46% for an S&P 500 fund on one 2026 comparison page, with the five-year figure slightly behind at 13.01% against 13.26% ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/mtum-vs-voo/)); a sister page with a different as-of date shows 15.79% and 11.03% ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/rsp-vs-iwm-vs-vtv-vs-schd-vs-mtum-vs-usmv-vs-vug-vs-qual/)). SPMO's 19.7% a year against 12.8% over the five years to August 2026 came with about 54% of the fund in technology ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/18/spmo-owns-the-sp-500s-100-fastest-rising-stocks-its-momentum-screen-has-beaten-the-index-by-67-points-over-the-last-five-years/)). The gap to the index depends on the end date, and in the last decade it mostly measured concentration in the same mega-cap leaders that drove the index.

The construction details with evidence behind them are few. A stricter rule to enter than to stay (buy in the top 10%, hold until the stock leaves the top 20%) cut momentum's monthly turnover from 34.5% to 18.8% and lifted its net long-short return from 0.68% to 0.85% a month ([Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). Momentum's half-life is about three months and it starts to lose money after about eight months of holding, with "fresh" winners far better than stocks that have already been winners for a year ([Research Affiliates via etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)). Residual momentum, which ranks on the part of return not explained by the Fama-French factors over months t−12 to t−2, scaled by its own volatility, had a Sharpe of 0.60 against 0.36 for plain momentum in the largest stocks over 1926–2009 ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/residual-momentum-factor)). Winners that rose through many small up days kept drifting for about eight months against about two for winners that jumped ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/frog-in-the-pan-identifying-the-highest-quality-momentum-stocks/)); the exact formula for that "frog in the pan" measure could not be verified from the paper this session and must be checked against [Da, Gurun and Warachka](https://www3.nd.edu/%7Ezda/Frog.pdf) before coding. Momentum crashes are mainly a short-leg event, driven by rebounds in past losers (−91% in mid-1932 and more than −73% within three months in 2009 for the long-short factor), so a long-only book escapes the worst but still carries the full market drawdown ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/avoiding-momentum-crashes/)).

Andreas Clenow's published rule set combines all of this into the closest template for Kaibot: rank S&P 500 stocks weekly by the annualised slope of a 90-day exponential regression multiplied by its R-squared; exclude any stock below its 100-day average or with a move above 15% in the window; size each position at account value × 0.001 ÷ 20-day ATR; buy from the top of the list only while the index is above its 200-day average; sell when a stock leaves the top 20%, breaks its 100-day average, gaps more than 15% or leaves the index; trade on one fixed weekday and resize every second week ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/10493/andreas-clenow-momentum-strategy-using-framework-from-stocks-on-the-move-book/)). An independent implementer reports 10 to 30 holdings, a minimum account near $30,000, volatility well below the index, good behaviour in 2008 and returns about level with the S&P 500 ([TuringTrader](https://turingtrader-com.web.app/portfolios/clenow-stocks-on-the-move)). That is the honest shape of the best candidate: index-like return with a gentler ride. At the $5,000 end of Kaibot's range the equal-risk book needs fractional shares, which Alpaca restricts to day orders, so a small account must hold fewer names or re-submit its stops every morning.

Three further multi-day ideas merit one test each, with low expectations. All-time-high breakouts with a wide ATR trailing stop showed 19.3% a year and a Sharpe of 1.24 over 1983–2004 ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/trend-following-effect-in-stocks)), and a 2025 follow-up on more than 66,000 trades found that fewer than 7% of trades produce most of the profit, with a Russell 3000 portfolio earning about 15% gross against 11.2% for the market over 1991–2024 ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/all-time-high-trend-following)). That portfolio targets 30% volatility with up to 200% leverage across hundreds of positions, and its authors call the base version hard to run below $1 million ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/does-trend-following-still-work-on-stocks/)); with 5 to 20 unlevered positions the result depends on catching the rare big winners, so any test must report the distribution over random subsets of signals, never one equity curve. Post-earnings drift was real through 2012 but has faded: Martineau finds none in large stocks since 2006 ([Critical Finance Review](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0122)), a 2001–2024 test loses significance once microcaps are removed (t = 1.43) ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)), and the only window that has strengthened is trading days +2 to +6 after the announcement ([Kettell, McInnis and Zhao](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf)). Pullback rules of the Connors type (close above the 200-day average, two-day RSI below 10, exit on a close above the five-day average) show 17.84% a year and a Sharpe of 1.10 on Nasdaq 100 stocks in one blog test that states no costs or constituent method ([Backtest](https://backtest.substack.com/p/the-2-period-rsi-a-simple-system)), and CXO's review of the original book found no costs, no out-of-sample test and no data-mining correction ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=2679)). The academically defensible version of mean reversion is conditional: a composite of short-term signals earned about 12% a year gross and stayed significant up to roughly 55 bp per trade, but one of its five inputs is analyst revisions, which the free data set lacks ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/alpha-from-short-term-signals-may-survive-market-frictions/)).

### Intraday: one SPY rule survives scrutiny, and only at sub-basis-point cost

The SPY noise-band rule of Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon is the single intraday design worth a slot. For each minute it averages the absolute move from the 9:30 open to that minute over the previous 14 days, sets an upper boundary at the higher of today's open and yesterday's close multiplied by one plus that average move, checks only on the hour and half hour from 10:00, goes long above the boundary, and exits at the close or when price falls below the higher of the boundary and the session VWAP. The headline of 19.6% a year at a Sharpe of 1.33 (May 2007 to April 2024) uses volatility-targeted sizing with up to 4x leverage, long and short; the paper's own unlevered version earned **9.5% to 9.7% a year** with a 12% maximum drawdown, the long leg contributed a little over half, and 2016 and 2017 were losing years ([Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)). Two user-shared replications with the same leverage cap reached only 9.9% and 13.4% a year at Sharpe ratios of 0.49 and 0.60 ([QuantConnect backtest](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_fd22e7bd368af4d6bd31153c9f8c4dad.html); [QuantConnect backtest](https://quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_32f62726550888da5f2a06694ac37930.html)), and the authors' own firm reports a sibling strategy's long sleeve at 7.3% a year gross, still with up to 4x leverage, through January 2026 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/breaking-the-rules-of-intraday-trading/)). The research team's estimate for a long-only, unlevered version is **3% to 5% a year** before SPY-specific costs, with capital idle most of the day. The unlevered edge works out at about 2 bp per round trip (our arithmetic from the paper's trade count), so the rule is positive only if SPY fills cost a fraction of a basis point and is clearly negative at a generic 6 bp. Kaibot's existing last-30-minutes SPY trade is in effect the 15:30 check of this framework, so one test covers both.

The opening-range breakout on "stocks in play" should be tested only to decide whether to keep it. Its published 41.6% a year at a Sharpe of 2.81 (2016–2023) trades long and short at up to 4x leverage, charges $0.0035 a share commission and nothing for slippage, relies on ranking about 7,000 stocks by first-five-minute relative volume, and falls to 3.2% a year without that filter; the average trade above 100% relative volume is worth +0.08R, and three stocks contributed more than 300R each ([Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)). A replication over 2016 to December 2024 netted 176% against the paper's 1,637% ([Reddit thread, mirror](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/algotrading/comments/1hezqql/opening_range_breakout_for_stocks_in_play_code)), and measured stop-entry slippage on a similar rule was 0.47R at the median against a break-even of 0.23R ([QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/21585/breakout-entries-on-us-stocks-eat-most-of-the-edge-how-do-you-cut-entry-cost/)). Kaibot also cannot reproduce the ranking on free data: Alpaca's free plan serves full-market history only once it is 15 minutes old ([Alpaca market data](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api.md)), so consolidated volume for 9:30–9:35 arrives at about 9:50, and the paper's own table shows the Sharpe falling from 2.81 with a 5-minute range to 0.21 with 30 minutes. The team's estimate for a long-only, unlevered version is about zero or negative after spread and stop slippage.

### Overnight: real, about 4 bp a night, and already captured by holding

The overnight premium has persisted. From the same Elm Wealth data, SPY's overnight-only return works out at about 3.8 bp a night over thirty years and 4.0 bp over the five years to early 2025 (our arithmetic), but over those five years a dollar grew to $1.66 overnight-only against $2.07 for simply holding, at a lower Sharpe ([Elm Wealth](https://elmwealth.com/night-shift/)). Trading it is another matter: SPY's 717% gross overnight return over 1993–2020 became −32% after the historical spread and a one-cent commission ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/)), and the one live product, NightShares 500, lost 6.9% while the S&P 500 gained 22% between June 2022 and July 2023 ([WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/etfs/-night-effect-etfs-to-shut-down-with-overnight-stock-returns-elusive)). The practical conclusion is that a multi-day book already owns every night at no extra turnover, and an unconditional overnight trade deserves no slot. The one conditional variant with support is to carry the SPY rule's open long to the next open: in the authors' gross test that lifted the long sleeve from 7.3% to 13.4% a year and its Sharpe from 0.76 to about 0.97 ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/breaking-the-rules-of-intraday-trading/)).

### What to test, exactly, and what to expect

Every "realistic expectation" below is the research team's estimate, net of about 6 bp per round trip and relative to SPY over the same period; no source reports long-only, unlevered, concentrated versions directly.

| Low-volatility stocks outperform | Risk-adjusted alpha at a beta well under 1; USMV returned 10.54% a year over ten years ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/rsp-vs-iwm-vs-vtv-vs-schd-vs-mtum-vs-usmv-vs-vug-vs-qual/)) | About 5 points a year behind the S&P 500 in the last decade |
| Dual momentum (12-month rotation) is a robust rule | One lucky lookback among 1,226 tested; the gap between the luckiest and unluckiest specification averages 64 points over five years ([ReSolve](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1110908075333038081.html)) | Use an ensemble of lookbacks or nothing |
| Internal bar strength has a Sharpe near 3 | Trading at the very close that generates the signal; with next-open execution the Sharpe falls to between −0.27 and 0.36 ([arXiv 2306.12434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12434)) | Nothing for an engine that decides after the close |

The decade itself is the last caveat. An S&P 500 fund compounded at about 15% a year over the ten years to 2026 ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/mtum-vs-voo/)), led by a handful of mega-cap growth stocks. Any defensive or diversifying rule had to lag in that window, and momentum kept up only by owning the same leaders. Every candidate should therefore be compared with SPY total return and with a random-entry control of equal holding period and exposure; CXO found that during the 2000s even random monthly timing tended to beat buy and hold, which is why a control matters ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/10-month-sma-timing-signals-over-the-short-run/)).

## How durable systematic traders work: few rules, small bets, halved Sharpe ratios

### Process starts from a reason and ends with a pre-written exit

The practitioners with long records describe the same sequence. Carver begins with a plausible idea and calibrates only small variations, fits on an expanding window so each year is tested with rules fitted on earlier years, and refuses to pick a single best variation, blending them instead ([the7circles on Carver](https://the7circles.uk/?p=29002)). Kevin Davey sets objectives first, prefers simple strategies with longer holds, runs walk-forward testing once against pass criteria fixed in advance, resamples trades by Monte Carlo to see the range of drawdowns, and then "incubates" a strategy without money for six to twelve months ([Algo Advantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about)); another summary of his book gives three to six months and adds a stop-trading threshold defined per strategy before it goes live ([FinancialWisdomTV](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/building-winning-algorithmic-trading-systems-kevin-davey-s-complete-framework-for-trading-success)). The institutional version, from Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz, asks for an economic hypothesis stated in advance (including who is on the other side), a count of everything tried, no convenient data exclusions, recognition that only live trading is truly out of sample, simple models, and no tweaking on live results ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2018/12/protocol-to-prevent-quants-gone-wild)). Man AHL publishes its trend research as a blend of all speeds because the fastest and slowest correlate at only 0.17 and no single speed is best ([Man Institute](https://www.man.com/maninstitute/need-for-speed-trend-following)). Perry Kaufman layers many simple strategies to obtain an average result, which he trusts more than a single backtested winner ([Algo Advantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/a-wealth-of-experience-trading-diversified)).

There is one real disagreement. Davey rotates strategies monthly on performance, while Carver and the Arnott protocol warn against dropping or adjusting rules on recent results; Carver's April 2026 review calls his own discretionary move to cash a mistake ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2026/04/annual-performance-update-year-12.html)). The synthesis that respects both is never to tune a live rule on live results, and to write the statistical retirement trigger (for example, a live drawdown outside the backtest's simulated range) before the rule starts trading.

### Portfolio construction is sizing by risk, not by conviction

Carver links the volatility target to the expected Sharpe ratio through the Kelly criterion and then halves it: a Sharpe of 0.5 justifies at most 25% annual volatility, a simple one-rule system about 12%, and most professional managers run 10% to 30% ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/03/how-much-risk-should-we-take.html)). Each position gets an equal slice of risk through inverse-volatility sizing; in his tests, dropping volatility scaling from a trend system cut the Sharpe from 0.92 to 0.57 ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2018/07/vol-targeting-and-trend-following.html)). Rules are combined with fixed, "handcrafted" weights that depend only on how the rules group and correlate, because formal optimisation overfits; momentum gets four or five speeds, and pairs of variations correlated at 0.95 or more are pruned to one ([the7circles on Carver](https://the7circles.uk/?p=29002)). His own system runs about 45 instruments, eight rules and 30 variations for a backtested Sharpe near 1.0 after costs over 35 years ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/a-few-notes-on-systematic-trading)). Diversification has limits that matter for Kaibot: constituents of the main CTA index still correlate at about 50% on average ([CFM](https://www.cfm.com/steady-trends-the-reality-of-cta-return-dispersion/)), and all long-only stock rules share market beta, so the benefit of combining them must be measured on returns in excess of the index. For an unlevered account, volatility targeting reduces to a dial between cash and fully invested.

### Risk control lives in sizing and portfolio limits, not in tight stops

Stops suit trend rules and wide thresholds. Adding a 10% stop to monthly long-short momentum improved its worst month from −49.79% to −11.34% and more than doubled its Sharpe ([Quant Investing](https://www.quant-investing.com/blog/truths-about-stop-losses-that-nobody-wants-to-believe)), whereas stops on mean-reversion entries subtract value, as Kaibot found. Kaufman avoids individual stops and exits on portfolio-level volatility extremes ([Algo Advantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/a-wealth-of-experience-trading-diversified)); Clenow handles gap risk by excluding stocks with recent 15% moves and sizing by ATR, and blocks new buys below the index's 200-day average. Drawdowns remain normal even for the most diversified systematic style: the SG Trend Index has had 16 drawdowns above 10% since 2000 and fell 20.4% between May 2024 and May 2025 ([Cambridge Associates](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/?p=19140)). Operational risk needs automation: Knight Capital's main risk monitor was watched by people, raised no automatic alerts and was not wired to order entry ([SEC order](https://contracts.justia.com/companies/kcg-holdings-inc-18985/contract/555902)).

### Validation discounts everything, and counts every attempt

Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue that after hundreds of published factors a new one should clear a t-ratio of about 3.0, not 2.0 ([NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). Harvey and Liu's worked example shows a Sharpe of 0.75 over twenty years shrinking to about 0.32 once 200 tried strategies are accounted for ([Harvey and Liu](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf)). CFM's model of iterative "improvement" concludes that backtests of strategies with typical Sharpe ratios of 0.3 to 0.5 should be discounted by about half ([arXiv 1902.01802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.01802)), and published anomalies themselves returned 26% less out of sample and 58% less after publication ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/effects-of-market-adaptation)). A hold-out set does not solve the problem, because it ignores how many trials preceded the selection ([Bailey et al.](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)); the deflated Sharpe ratio corrects a reported figure for the number of trials and for non-normal returns ([Bailey and López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)), and purged or combinatorial cross-validation removes training data that overlaps test labels and yields a distribution of outcomes where walk-forward gives one path ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)). The mechanical errors are well catalogued: fitting statistics on the full sample, using a bar's high or low before it has closed, testing only survivors ([QuantStart, part I](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Successful-Backtesting-of-Algorithmic-Trading-Strategies-Part-I/)), and assuming fills at prices that were never obtainable ([QuantStart, part II](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Successful-Backtesting-of-Algorithmic-Trading-Strategies-Part-II)).

Over roughly 2014 to March 2026 the SG CTA Index ran at a Sharpe of 0.54 and Carver's own leveraged, cross-asset futures account at 0.80 before deducting a risk-free rate ([Carver](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2026/04/annual-performance-update-year-12.html)). Those results come from cross-asset diversification, shorting and leverage, none of which Kaibot has; they are a ceiling for process quality, not a return forecast.

Kaibot's scale does not need NautilusTrader's speed. It needs NautilusTrader's discipline, which can be copied: a risk gate between strategy and venue, a single-threaded core fed by a message bus, one kernel shared by backtest, sandbox and live with the clock injected, reconciliation with the venue before trading starts, and a crash-and-restart model under a supervisor ([NautilusTrader architecture](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/architecture/)).

### The target shape: one decision core, swappable edges, targets for slow rules

All the serious engines converge on the same pipeline of data, strategy, portfolio construction, risk, execution and broker adapter, driven by a clock ([QuantStart](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Event-Driven-Backtesting-with-Python-Part-I)). The rebuilt Kaibot should put strategies, portfolio construction, the risk gate and the order state machine in a pure core with no input or output; define Broker, MarketData, Clock, Store and Notifier as interfaces; and supply two sets of adapters, real (Alpaca, wall clock, Supabase) and simulated (a simulated broker, a simulated clock, Parquet data, an in-memory store). The backtester then becomes the live engine with different adapters, and the separate rule implementation is deleted. The simulated broker must enforce Alpaca's actual rules, so that parity failures surface in simulation instead of in production: auction submission windows, bracket restrictions, day-only fractional orders, 90-day expiry of good-till-cancelled orders, and stops that become market orders.

LEAN's framework separates a signal from a target holding from an order, and its own documentation says the target model suits rebalancing and ranking strategies while entry-and-exit rules fit it poorly ([LEAN framework](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/algorithm-framework/overview)). That maps onto Kaibot's two families. Multi-day rules should run as a target-reconciliation loop: once per decision time, compute target shares per symbol, subtract broker-confirmed positions and working orders, and send only the difference. Such a loop heals itself after a missed fill, a restart or a manual trade. Intraday rules keep signal-triggered bracket orders, which do not heal themselves and need explicit state recovery. Both paths must pass through the same risk gate and the same order manager. Three parity tests follow from this structure (engineering proposals, not sourced practice): replay a recorded day and compare the emitted order intents with a stored file; replay yesterday's live bars through the simulator each morning and compare its decisions with the live log; and require two runs of the same replay to produce identical logs, which forbids wall-clock reads and unseeded randomness inside strategy code.

### Order management: the broker is the truth, and an unknown order is not a failed order

NautilusTrader's reconciliation rules give the checklist. Before strategies start, the engine pulls order, fill and position reports, aligns its own records with them, adopts orders it does not recognise as external, and refuses to start while positions are unresolved; at run time, an order that stays unconfirmed is queried individually before any conclusion is drawn, a stale snapshot never undoes a later fill, and a missing position report "is not evidence of flat" ([NautilusTrader reconciliation](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/execution/reconciliation)). Alpaca's documentation supplies the matching primitives: a client order ID of up to 128 characters by which an order can be fetched, and a warning that after a timeout the order may already be at the market, so it must be neither resent nor marked cancelled until confirmed ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/working-with-orders.md)). The pattern for Kaibot is to write an order intent with a deterministic ID to the database first, submit with that ID, and after any timeout or crash look the order up by ID before doing anything else. The order state machine needs an explicit "unknown" state that blocks new orders in that symbol until a targeted query resolves it. Every broker event should be stored append-only, with the decision record (inputs, configuration version, code version) that produced each intent; positions are derived from broker reports and checked against the fill ledger.

Alpaca's order rules shape multi-day trading in specific ways ([Alpaca orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca)). Fractional orders are day-only, so a resting stop requires whole shares. Good-till-cancelled orders are cancelled 90 days after creation, so protective stops need a re-arming job. Bracket exit legs arm only after the entry fills completely, so a partly filled entry can leave shares unprotected. Bracket legs are not adjusted for corporate actions, and trailing stops can be cancelled or re-priced around splits at Alpaca's discretion, so the engine should cancel and re-create protective orders around split dates (how a plain stop is treated on a split is not documented and needs confirming with Alpaca). A triggered stop is a market order, so overnight gap risk has to be absorbed by position size.

### Operations: Knight Capital's lessons at the scale of one machine

On 1 August 2012 Knight's router sent more than 4 million orders while trying to fill 212 customer orders and lost over $460 million in 45 minutes ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)). Retired code had been left on servers behind a flag that was later reused, a deployment missed one of eight servers with no second reviewer, 97 warning emails went unread before the open, and the response without a runbook spread the fault to the healthy servers ([Varma](https://www.jrvarma.in/blog/Y2013/Knight-Capital.html)). The futures industry's guide turns this into controls: a kill switch that cancels working orders, heartbeat monitoring, restricted risk limits after any substantive change, maximum order size and position limits enforced before the order leaves, market-data sanity checks, a breaker on repeated automated executions, and closing trades allowed even when a limit is breached ([FIA guide](https://www.fia.org/sites/default/files/2019-09/FIA-Guide-to-the-Development-and-Operation-of-Automated-Trading-Systems.pdf)).

For Kaibot these become concrete. Only one container image, identified by its git commit, trades, and the engine records that identifier with every decision and refuses to start if it differs from the registered release. Retired strategies are deleted, not hidden behind flags. A sanity governor outside strategy code halts trading when orders per minute or open notional exceed hard caps. A kill switch exists in three forms: a flag in the database read every loop, a flatten-and-halt command, and a path that does not depend on the engine. A dead-man alarm pings an external monitor during market hours; Healthchecks.io's free plan allows 20 checks with email and chat alerts ([Healthchecks.io](https://healthchecks.io/docs/healthchecks_dead_mans_snitch_comparison)). Scheduling comes from Alpaca's calendar and clock endpoints, which carry holidays and early closes ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/reference/querymarketcalendar-1)). The project index shows that Kaibot already takes a database lease at start-up, runs a watchdog container and deploys outside market hours; one caution applies to the lease, because a session-level Postgres advisory lock misbehaves silently behind a transaction-mode connection pooler ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/mukesh_13/postgres-advisory-locks-for-distributed-cron-killing-duplicate-job-runs-without-a-redis-dependency-1bfl)). Since protective stops rest at the broker, an engine outage leaves open positions protected, which is a reason to keep broker-side stops on every overnight holding.

Testing follows the same layering (engineering proposals built on the FIA guide). Property tests assert the risk gate's invariants, such as no order above the notional cap and a daily-loss halt that still permits closing orders. A fake broker scripts partial fills, rejects, timeouts followed by late fills, duplicate events and auction-window rejections. Start-up tests confront the engine with positions and orders it has never seen. Each release soaks on the paper account with a daily comparison of the engine's ledger against the broker. Outage drills kill the process mid-order and cut the network, and pass only with zero duplicate orders.

### Research runs in two tiers, and only the engine's numbers count

Quant teams commonly screen ideas in a fast vectorised layer and confirm survivors in the event-driven engine; a 500-ticker, ten-year daily vectorised test finishes in under a second, while event-driven minute data for the same universe takes 15 to 30 minutes ([IBKR Campus](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/quant-news/a-practical-breakdown-of-vector-based-vs-event-based-backtesting/)). Minute bars belong in Parquet files partitioned by year and queried in place with DuckDB, in files of 100 MB to 10 GB with row groups of 100,000 to a million rows ([DuckDB](https://duckdb.org/docs/2.0/guides/performance/file_formats.html)). The rule that keeps two tiers honest is that the vectorised layer only ranks ideas; any number used for a go-live decision comes from the engine running with the simulated broker. Every run, including failures, is written to a register with its code version, configuration, data snapshot, universe and cost model, because the count of trials is an input to every honest statistic.

## Six basis points is about right in total and wrong in its parts

### What a small marketable order really costs

The best public evidence comes from execution-quality reports for January 2019 to March 2022: retail-size marketable orders handled by wholesalers paid a full effective spread of **4.62 bp in S&P 500 stocks**, 56% of the quoted spread, and 15.96 bp in the next-largest size group ([Dyhrberg, Shkilko and Werner](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f187847/f187847.pdf)). A round trip therefore costs about 4.6 bp in spread for an average S&P 500 name, roughly double Kaibot's 2.2 bp. The 3.1 bp impact term errs in the other direction. A $14,000 order is 0.028% of daily volume at a $50 million floor, measured impact is linear and below the square-root curve for orders under about a thousandth of daily volume ([Bucci et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05230)), and the classic impact calibration excluded orders below 0.25% of daily volume altogether ([Almgren et al.](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/costestim.pdf)). The research team's estimate is 0.5 to 1.5 bp of impact at the liquidity floor and 0.1 to 0.5 bp for typical large caps; the "price impact" column in the execution-quality data is a component of the effective spread, so charging both double counts. Explicit fees are small: the SEC's sale fee is $20.60 per million dollars from 4 April 2026 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/fee-rate-advisories/2026-2)) and FINRA's is $0.000195 a share in 2026 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rule-filings/sr-finra-2024-019/fee-adjustment-schedule)), about a quarter of a basis point per round trip on a $14,000 position (our arithmetic).

The sum of about 6 bp is therefore reasonable for an average S&P 500 stock, low for mid caps and high for the most liquid mega caps. No Alpaca-specific execution-quality figure was found. In a controlled experiment at five other brokers, the share of the spread returned as price improvement ranged from 18.8% to 47.2% depending on the broker ([Schwarz et al., slides](https://cqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Huang_Actual-Retail-Price_CQA.pdf)), and because Alpaca keeps 12% of the spread, the prudent assumption is fills at 60% to 100% of the quoted half-spread until Alpaca's own report under the amended disclosure rule, in force since 1 August 2026, is checked ([SEC](https://sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/09/disclosure-order-execution-information)). The replacement cost model below is the research team's interpolation, not a published schedule.

| Liquidity bucket (average daily dollar volume) | Half effective spread per side | Impact per side | Fees (sell side) | Per round trip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Over $500 million | 1 to 2.5 bp | 0 to 0.3 bp | 0.25 bp | about 3 to 6 bp |
| $100 million to $500 million | 2.5 to 5 bp | 0.3 to 0.5 bp | 0.25 bp | about 6 to 11 bp |
| $50 million to $100 million | 5 to 8 bp | 0.5 to 1 bp | 0.25 bp | about 11 to 18 bp |

Better than buckets is a per-stock estimate of the quoted spread, charged at 60% to 100% of the half-spread per side. Every result should be reported at one, two and three times the base cost together with its break-even cost. For holding periods of days to weeks these costs are small against typical moves; the larger threats to honesty are survivorship, look-ahead in close-based signals and gap fills.

### Order types, time of day and the paper simulator

Alpaca offers market, limit, stop, stop-limit and trailing-stop orders, bracket and one-cancels-other classes, and day, good-till-cancelled, opening-auction and closing-auction instructions; its documentation describes no protective collar on market orders ([Alpaca orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca.md)). The pattern-day-trader rule no longer constrains anything: Alpaca removed day-trade counting on 4 June 2026 ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/blog/finra-retires-the-pdt-rule-introducing-alpacas-new-intraday-margin-framework/)).

Time of day matters more than order type. The closing auction handled about 9% of US volume on a typical 2024 day ([BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)), and for large NYSE stocks a trade worth 1% of daily volume moved the price 4.5 bp at the close against 9.1 bp in continuous trading, while the opening auction, at about 1.6% of volume, is the thinnest venue of the day ([Goyal, Jegadeesh and Wu](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/25551_Price_Impact.pdf)). Spreads are widest after the open and settle by about 10:00 ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/best-times-etf-investors-trade/)). Closing prices usually print at the pre-close bid or ask ([Bogousslavsky and Muravyev](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v66y2023ics1386418123000502.html)), so the close is cheap but not free; half a spread per side is the conservative backtest setting. The recommended practice is a marketable limit a few basis points through the quote after about 9:45 for intraday decisions, a closing-auction order in whole shares submitted before Alpaca's 15:50 cut-off for end-of-day decisions, and no opening-auction orders unless the rule's edge depends on the open, in which case that fill is charged at least twice the midday half-spread. A close-based signal must be computed from a price a few minutes before the cut-off and filled at the official close, or filled the next day; computing it from the close and filling at the same close is look-ahead.

Paper results need the same scepticism. Alpaca's simulator fills marketable orders against the live best bid and offer without checking size, fills resting limits with no queue, and models no price improvement, market impact, fees or dividends ([Alpaca paper trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)). For marketable orders the price is roughly fair; for limit orders it is optimistic; and multi-day paper profit omits dividends, so it will trail a backtest on adjusted prices by roughly the dividend yield. The free data plan is adequate for daily decisions (full-market history older than 15 minutes, live quotes from IEX only, 30 streaming symbols, history from 2016), and the $99 plan matters only for intraday pricing ([Alpaca market data](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api.md)).

### The data repairs that make multi-day backtests honest

Survivorship is the largest hazard. Kaibot's universe of 300 of today's large caps is close to the worst case for any rule that buys strength, because today's large caps are by construction the decade's winners. One reported pair of experiments shows a Nasdaq 100 momentum rule falling from a 46% compound annual return to 16.4%, with its maximum drawdown rising from 41% to 83%, once delisted members were included; the figures come from a trading-education site relaying another author's tests and are illustrative only ([Quantified Strategies via Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/stock-trading/news/survivorship-free-momentum-and-trend-following-strategies-3e520ccc-26a2-4c5c-bdee-52299ea573f0/)). Free point-in-time S&P 500 membership can be rebuilt from Wikipedia's change table, which one reconstruction judges reliable back to about 2000 ([Robot Wealth](https://robotwealth.com/how-to-get-historical-spx-constituents-data-for-free/)), cross-checked against dated iShares holdings files that begin in October 2006 ([Riaz Arbi](https://riazarbi.github.io/quant/backtesting-sp500-constituent-history)). Alpaca staff say delisted symbols remain in the database, but there is no historical-assets endpoint, forum users report gaps, and post-delisting over-the-counter prices are unavailable to ordinary accounts ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/delisted-tickers/18227); [Alpaca market data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq)). The bars endpoint's as-of parameter lets an old ticker be queried on a past date ([Alpaca bars reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars)). The build is therefore: membership by date, bars requested as of a date inside each membership spell, a log of every symbol that returns nothing, holes filled from another daily source or booked at a conservative terminal return, and the liquidity screen applied using only data available on each date. Every candidate should then run on both today's 300 and the point-in-time list, with the gap reported; a rule that works only on the former is rejected.

Earnings dates need a point-in-time source. Free calendars are shallow (Finnhub's free tier reportedly covers one month of calendar and four quarters of surprises ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/d/data-providers/finnhub/))), and vendor feeds that keep only the latest record create look-ahead ([QVeris](https://qveris.ai/guides/free-earnings-calendar-api/)). The defensible free timestamp is the filing time of the earnings 8-K on EDGAR: in one sample of 30 companies every firm furnished it shortly after the close on the day of the release, with dates announced about three weeks ahead ([JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/snapshot-earnings-date-announcements-3291029)). EDGAR's access details were not verified this session. The announcement session is the first regular session that opens after the timestamp, no decision may use that session's close before it has happened, and archiving daily snapshots of the free calendars from now creates a true point-in-time record at no cost. Rules that need historical surprise magnitudes are hard to test honestly on free data; rules that need only the date are feasible.

Corporate actions complete the list. Alpaca's historical bars are raw by default and can be adjusted for splits, dividends and spin-offs on request, while latest-price endpoints are always raw ([Alpaca bars reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbars)). Signals that compare prices across days (returns, averages, breakouts, ATR) must use fully adjusted data, and anything sent to the market (share counts, stop levels, dollar-volume screens) must use raw prices. Adjusted history is restated whenever a new dividend or split occurs, so the safe design stores raw bars with adjustment factors and adjusts in code. In backtests a stop that is gapped through fills at the open, never at the stop price.

## Data and harness first, then tests, then the order manager

The order of work follows from one principle: nothing is tested until the test can be trusted, and nothing is built for a rule that has not passed. Research screening runs in the vectorised layer; go or no-go numbers come only from the engine with the simulated broker.

| Order | Work | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Freeze the dip rule and stop tuning it; register every variant ever run (v1, A, B, the tuned configurations, the five variations); keep the paper engine running purely as a plumbing soak test | An honest trial count; no more capital of attention on a rule the evidence has closed |
| 2 | Honest data: point-in-time index membership, as-of bar requests with a hole log, raw bars plus adjustment factors, EDGAR earnings timestamps, daily calendar snapshots | Whether results survive on stocks that were actually investable; the survivorship gap as a number |
| 3 | Cost and fill model v2: per-stock half-spread by liquidity, near-zero impact, 0.25 bp fees, open fills at double cost, gapped stops at the open, pre-cut-off prices for close signals | Results at 1x, 2x and 3x cost and a break-even cost for every rule |
| 4 | Validation harness: a one-page pre-registration per rule (hypothesis, who is on the other side, frozen parameters, pass marks), SPY total-return benchmark, random-entry control, expanding-window evaluation, 50% Sharpe haircut, parameter-plateau check | Pass or fail decided before the result is seen |
| 5 | One engine, two brokers: Broker and Clock interfaces, a simulated broker that enforces Alpaca's rules, deletion of the separate simulator, golden-file replays; reproduce the dip rule's ten-year result within tolerance | Backtest and live are the same code |
| 6 | Core test: Clenow-style trend-filtered momentum and banded 12-1 momentum, 2016 to 2026, including late 2018, March 2020 and 2022 | Whether a multi-day core exists and what it looks like against SPY |
| 7 | SPY noise band, long only, then the overnight carry; measure Kaibot's real SPY fill cost alongside | Whether one intraday rule earns its place; retires or absorbs the last-30-minutes trade |
| 8 | One-shot, low-prior tests: breakout keep-or-delete, one-week post-earnings window, pullback in uptrend, all-time-high breakout with subset distributions | Deletes rules; anything that passes is a bonus |
| 9 | Order manager and operations for whatever passed: order intents with deterministic IDs, state machine, start-up and periodic reconciliation, target-reconciliation loop, stop re-arming, corporate-action checks, sanity governor, kill switches, release gate, outage drills | An engine that can hold positions for weeks without supervision |
| 10 | Paper incubation for three to six months at minimum, with tolerance bands and a retirement trigger written beforehand | Whether live behaviour matches the backtest's range |

Steps 2 to 4 come first because every multi-day result depends on them. Step 5 can proceed in parallel. The reliability parts of step 9 (idempotent orders, reconciliation, the release gate) are worth building even if no rule passes, because the engine runs every day regardless.

The honest forecast has four parts. First, the dip rule is finished and the opening-range breakout will very probably be deleted. Second, the SPY noise-band rule has a fair chance of passing at SPY's true costs, and if it does it adds low single digits a year on capital that is otherwise idle. Third, the momentum core will most likely deliver roughly index-like returns, somewhere between two points behind and one or two points ahead of SPY a year, with visibly shallower drawdowns when the trend filter is on and multi-year stretches behind the index. Fourth, the test cannot prove more than that. The standard error of an annual Sharpe ratio is roughly one over the square root of the number of years, about 0.32 at ten years (our arithmetic), so an excess-return Sharpe of 0.2 or 0.3 over SPY is statistically invisible in this sample, and a backtested Sharpe above about 1.0 for a long-only stock system should be read as a warning of overfitting. A realistic live target after the haircut is a total-return Sharpe of 0.4 to 0.7, at least one year in three flat or behind the index, and drawdowns of 20% or more of the strategy's own equity curve (research team's calibration). Alpaca's history starts in 2016, so the sample contains one bear market of note; adding earlier daily history from another source would help, but free coverage was not verified.

## Conclusion

The move to multi-day holding relocates Kaibot's problem. With the dip rule the constraint was cost, and ten thousand trades gave enough statistical power to convict the rule beyond doubt. With slow rules cost nearly vanishes and statistical power goes with it: a monthly-rebalanced portfolio produces too few independent bets in ten years to confirm a small edge. The existing go-live bar of 900 trades at t above 3 was written for day trading and cannot be met by a slow portfolio. Its replacement has to rest on something other than a t-statistic from Kaibot's own sample: decades of published evidence for the rule's family, a frozen specification, a ten-year run that fails to contradict that evidence on investable stocks at stressed costs, and paper behaviour inside pre-declared bands. The ten-year test becomes a falsification tool, and it is good at that job.

The second shift is in what counts as the default. Holding SPY captures the overnight premium, pays no spread and has beaten the defensive overlays reviewed here on return since 2009. Each rule should be framed as a deviation from that holding which must earn its place, on return or on drawdown, against a pass mark set in advance. The most durable asset v3 can produce is an engine whose reported numbers are the numbers of the code that trades, on stocks that were investable, at costs that were payable. Three measurements would change this assessment and are cheap to obtain: Kaibot's actual fill cost in SPY, the performance gap between today's universe and the point-in-time one, and Alpaca's broker-level execution report under the disclosure regime that began in August 2026.
