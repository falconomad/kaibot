## 1. Cross-sectional momentum in stocks (12-1, 52-week high, industry, residual, frog-in-the-pan; crashes and mitigation)

### Takeaway
Stock momentum (12-month return skipping the latest month) is the best-replicated price anomaly and survives costs on paper, but the premium is roughly half as large after publication, is weaker in large caps, and live long-only funds have captured little of it; the long-only large-cap versions (MTUM, SPMO) have been roughly index-like over 2016-2026 with large swings depending on the end date. Crashes are mainly a short-leg (loser rebound) problem, so a long-only book avoids the worst of them but still carries full market drawdown.

### Cited Findings

Construction and gross/net size of the premium
- Standard momentum signal = past 12-month return skipping the most recent month. Long-short UMD earned 8.3%/yr with Sharpe 0.50 over 1927-2013 (gross). — [AQR, Fact, Fiction and Momentum Investing](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- Market-adjusted, the long (winner) side contributed 5.5%/yr and the short side 5.1%/yr over 1927-2013, i.e. about half the premium is available long-only. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- Momentum is weaker in large caps: 6.8%/yr in large caps vs 9.8%/yr in small caps (full sample, long-short); over 1991-2013 the UMD Sharpe ratio was only 0.24 in large caps vs 0.45 in small caps. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- Novy-Marx and Velikov (sample 07/1963-12/2012, long-short deciles, all figures % per month): momentum gross 1.33 (t 4.80), one-sided turnover 34.5%/month, trading costs 0.65, net 0.68 (t 2.45). With a 10%/20% buy/hold rule (enter in top decile, hold until it leaves the top 20%): gross 1.20, turnover 18.8%, costs 0.35, net 0.85 (t 3.35). — [Novy-Marx and Velikov, A Taxonomy of Anomalies and their Trading Costs](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)
- The buy/hold spread (stricter rule to enter than to stay) is the single most effective cost reducer: across 23 anomalies it cut turnover about 41% and costs about 42%. — [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)
- Industry momentum does NOT survive costs in that study: gross 0.93%/month, turnover 90.1%/month, costs 1.22, net -0.29 (t -1.20); with 10/50 buy/hold net -0.02. — [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)
- Institutional cost estimates are much lower: Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz, using live trade data, find actual costs "an order of magnitude smaller" than earlier studies and a long-short momentum break-even capacity of $56B; Novy-Marx and Velikov's capacity estimate is about $5.8B. — [Alpha Architect, transaction costs summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/transaction-costs/)
- Momentum's annual turnover is five to six times that of value. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en)

Post-publication and recent performance
- Momentum premium was 10.9%/yr pre-publication (1927-1993) and 5.5%/yr post-publication (1994-2015); 2010-2015 it was 6.8%/yr (Fama-French UMD, long-short, gross). Data ends 2015. — [Swedroe, Is Momentum Really Dead? (etf.com)](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-really-dead)
- Research Affiliates (2017): US standard momentum (long-short, gross) was still below its 1999 peak, i.e. about 18 years with no alpha before costs; the factor earned about 5.0%/yr from 1990 and just over 3.0%/yr after the 2009 crash. — [Research Affiliates via etf.com, Can Momentum Investing Be Saved?](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)
- Live funds: US mutual funds with "momentum" in the name had value-add of -2.2%/yr (sample-weighted), CAPM alpha -2.6%/yr; no US momentum-named fund had cumulatively beaten its benchmark since inception net of fees (as of 2017). Funds "capture essentially none" of the paper premium. — [Research Affiliates via etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)
- Signal decay: momentum's half-life is about three months; it starts losing money after about eight months of holding. "Fresh" momentum (recent winners that were not already winners) peaks about 7% by month 11; "stale" momentum peaks just over 1% around month 5 and loses about 8% by month 36 (gross, hypothetical). — [Research Affiliates via etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)
- Long-only ETF evidence, MTUM (MSCI USA Momentum: large/mid caps ranked on risk-adjusted 6- and 12-month returns, reconstituted semiannually in May and November, fee 0.15%): through 16 June 2026 MTUM returned 391% over 10 years vs 325% for SPY, 104% vs 89% over 5 years, and 30.5% vs 10.3% year-to-date 2026. — [24/7 Wall St, 17 June 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/06/17/forget-the-plain-sp-500-the-momentum-version-captured-the-run-and-beat-it-by-15-this-year/)
- Same comparison at a different (unstated, 2026) as-of date: MTUM 10-year annualized 16.50% vs VOO 15.46%; 5-year 13.01% vs 13.26% (MTUM behind); 1-year 25.67% vs 18.00%. — [StockAnalysis MTUM vs VOO](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/mtum-vs-voo/)
- A third page (also undated, 2026) shows MTUM 10-year 15.79%, 5-year 11.03%, 1-year 15.81%. The three sources disagree because their as-of dates differ, which itself shows how end-date sensitive the momentum-vs-index gap is. — [StockAnalysis multi-ETF comparison](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/rsp-vs-iwm-vs-vtv-vs-schd-vs-mtum-vs-usmv-vs-vug-vs-qual/)
- SPMO (S&P 500 Momentum: about 100 S&P 500 stocks with the highest momentum scores, weighted by market cap times momentum score, rebalanced March and September, fee 0.13%, about 44% annual turnover): through 31 Aug 2026, 5-year annualized 19.7% vs 12.8% for the S&P 500; 3-year 37.3% vs 21.0%; YTD 23.8% vs 13.1%. Portfolio was about 54% technology with the top 10 holdings just over half the fund. — [24/7 Wall St, 18 Sept 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/18/spmo-owns-the-sp-500s-100-fastest-rising-stocks-its-momentum-screen-has-beaten-the-index-by-67-points-over-the-last-five-years/)

Variants
- 52-week high: signal PTH = current price / 52-week high; sort into deciles, long nearest the high. In a value-weighted all-stock test the high-minus-low spread is significant against CAPM, FF3, Carhart and FF5 but its alpha is insignificant under the q-factor model (it loads on ROE). The Alpha Architect author judges the signal less robust to small changes (e.g. holding period) than total-return momentum and prefers the latter. — [Alpha Architect, 52-week high post](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=37531)
- Industry 52-week-high (Hong, Jordan, Liu): PRILAG = price / 52-week high, cap-weighted to 20 industries, long top 6 industries, hold 3 months; long-short about 0.93%/month over 1963-2009, Sharpe about 0.7, gross of costs; Quantpedia notes out-of-sample backtests are slightly negative and most gains come from the long side. — [Quantpedia, 52-Weeks High Effect in Stocks](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/52-weeks-high-effect-in-stocks)
- Residual momentum (Blitz, Huij, Martens): each month regress each stock's returns on the Fama-French three factors over the past 36 months; sum the residuals over months t-12 to t-2; divide by the standard deviation of residuals over the same window; long top decile. Source abstract: risk-adjusted profits "about twice as large" as total-return momentum; in the largest-cap robustness test Sharpe 0.60 vs 0.36 for total-return momentum (long-short, gross, 1926-2009). Smaller time-varying factor exposure, so it suffered less in early 2009. — [Quantpedia, Residual Momentum Factor](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/residual-momentum-factor)
- Frog in the pan (Da, Gurun, Warachka; sample 1927-2007): among past winners, those whose gains came through many small positive days ("continuous information", % positive days > % negative days) keep drifting; six-month momentum return falls monotonically from 5.94% for continuous-information stocks to -2.07% for discrete-information stocks; continuous-information momentum stays significant about 8 months vs about 2 months for discrete. Long-short, gross. — [Alpha Architect, Frog in the Pan](https://alphaarchitect.com/frog-in-the-pan-identifying-the-highest-quality-momentum-stocks/)
- Replication status: 12-1 momentum, industry momentum, residual momentum and abnormal earnings-announcement returns all survive in Hou-Xue-Zhang's value-weighted, NYSE-breakpoint replication. — [Alpha Architect, Replicating Anomalies summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/)

Crashes and mitigation
- Daniel and Moskowitz (1927-2013): after big market declines the loser decile's beta can exceed 3 while winners' beta falls below 0.5, so long-short momentum has a large negative beta and crashes when the market rebounds; their dynamic scaling roughly doubles alpha and Sharpe of static momentum. — [Alpha Architect, Avoiding Momentum Crashes](https://alphaarchitect.com/avoiding-momentum-crashes/)
- Crash sizes (long-short): about -91% in June-August 1932; more than -73% within three months in 2009; smaller crashes 1938/39, 1974/75, 2001/02. Monthly skewness -2.3, kurtosis 16.6. Crashes "are driven by large gains of previous losers" while winners stay modestly positive. — [Alpha Architect, Avoiding Momentum Crashes](https://alphaarchitect.com/avoiding-momentum-crashes/)
- Worst full-sample drawdown: momentum -77%, value -43%, 60/40 value/momentum blend -30%. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- The 2002 and 2009 crashes cut the US momentum factor by 28% and 54%. — [Research Affiliates via etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)
- Barroso and Santa-Clara: scale momentum to a constant 12% volatility target using its own realized variance; this "virtually eliminated crashes and nearly doubled the Sharpe ratio" (long-short, requires leverage in calm periods). A crash-indicator variant cut the worst monthly loss from 77% to about 25%. — [Alpha Architect, Avoiding Momentum Crashes](https://alphaarchitect.com/avoiding-momentum-crashes/)
- Volatility-managed momentum is one of the few managed factors that held up out of sample in Cederburg et al. (MOM, ROE, BAB were the exceptions to real-time degradation, though they too weakened once Fama-French factors were controlled for), and the scaled position's 99th-percentile leverage reaches 864% for momentum. — [Alpha Architect summary of Cederburg et al.](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/); [Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang, Yan paper](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cost is not the binding constraint for Kaibot. At 34.5% one-sided monthly turnover and 6 bp per round trip, a long-only top-momentum book costs roughly 0.345 x 6 bp = about 2 bp/month (about 25 bp/yr); with a buy/hold band (18.8% turnover) about 14 bp/yr. The academic 0.65%/month cost figure reflects small caps and 1963-2012 spreads. The binding question is whether any gross alpha over SPY exists in liquid large caps.
- The honest long-only large-cap expectation is "index-like, plus or minus": about half of the premium is on the long side, large-cap momentum is weaker (Sharpe 0.24 long-short in 1991-2013), post-publication premium is about half, and live funds captured little. MTUM's 10-year edge over the S&P 500 ranges from about +0.3 to +1.0 pt/yr depending on the as-of date and was negative over one 5-year window; SPMO's large 2021-2026 lead coincides with a 54% technology weighting, i.e. it is a concentrated bet on the same leaders that drove the index.
- A 5-20 stock version will have far more tracking error than a 100-stock ETF. Expect multi-year stretches behind SPY.
- Useful engineering choices supported by the evidence: (a) entry/exit hysteresis (enter top 10%, exit below top 20-50%); (b) monthly or slower rebalance; (c) prefer "fresh" winners and smooth paths (FIP) over stocks that have already been winners for a year or jumped on one day; (d) residual momentum to reduce factor/sector whipsaw; (e) no individual-stock volatility leverage, but cutting gross exposure when realized volatility spikes or the index is below trend is the long-only analogue of crash mitigation.
- Frog-in-the-pan formula as I recall it from the paper (not verified this session because the PDF was blocked): ID = sign(PRET) x (% negative days - % positive days) over the 12-month formation window; lower ID = more continuous. Verify against the paper before coding: [Da, Gurun, Warachka](https://www3.nd.edu/%7Ezda/Frog.pdf).

### Gaps
- No fetched source gives the Fama-French UMD or a long-only top-decile large-cap return for exactly 2016-2026; the ETF data above are the best live proxy.
- George and Hwang's original monthly returns versus Jegadeesh-Titman and their top/bottom 30% construction could not be verified (paper PDF blocked).
- Daniel and Moskowitz's own statement on long-only winners during crashes was only seen via the Alpha Architect summary; no long-only crash drawdown figure was found.
- Barroso and Santa-Clara's volatility lookback window was not stated on the page fetched.
- Max drawdown and Sharpe for MTUM/SPMO over 2016-2026 were not on the pages fetched.

## 2. Time-series momentum / trend following on stocks and ETFs (Faber 10-month SMA, Clenow, Antonacci dual momentum, breakout/new-high systems)

### Takeaway
Trend rules reliably cut volatility and drawdown but have not beaten buy-and-hold on return since 2009 on the US index; breakout/all-time-high systems on single stocks show a positive, highly skewed edge in backtests (fewer than 7% of trades make the money) but the published results rely on hundreds of positions, volatility targeting with leverage and daily turnover, and the authors themselves say the base version is unworkable below about $1M.

### Cited Findings

Index/ETF trend filters
- Faber rule: hold an asset only when its price is above its 10-month simple moving average, otherwise hold cash; evaluated monthly; five ETFs equal-weighted (SPY, EFA, BND, VNQ, GSG) in the Quantpedia version; backtest starts 1973. — [Quantpedia, Asset Class Trend-Following](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/asset-class-trend-following)
- Faber out-of-sample on US stocks, 2006-2016: timing 8.5%/yr vs 7.7% for the S&P 500, volatility 7.4% vs 14.7%, max drawdown 16.7% vs 51%. But after the 2008-09 bear market the rule trailed stocks in six of the next eight years. Data ends 2016. — [Swedroe, Why Financial Trends Persist (etf.com)](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist)
- 200-day moving average on the S&P 500, data through 31 Dec 2024: outperformed buy-and-hold 1999-2010 with about half the volatility (daily s.d. 0.72% vs 1.36%), but has underperformed in every period since the financial crisis; in 2020 it exited during the crash, re-entered late and finished behind. Over 1999-2010 it switched 47 times of which about 45 were false signals. — [Of Dollars and Data, Why Trend Following is Harder Than it Looks (Mar 2025)](https://ofdollarsanddata.com/why-trend-following-is-harder-than-it-looks/)
- Long-run test (S&P Composite 1860-2014, 0.25% one-way cost, out-of-sample from 1870): moving-average/momentum timing had higher Sharpe than the market over the full period, but in 1932-2014 Sharpe was only 7-14% higher and not statistically superior; price-vs-SMA rules underperformed from the early 1930s to late 1960s and from the late 1970s to early 2000s; rules were only 1-3% more accurate than buy-and-hold at identifying bull/bear states. Data ends 2014. — [CXO Advisory, Long-run Moving Average Horse Race](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-run-moving-average-horse-race-for-timing-the-u-s-stock-market)
- SPY 1993-Sept 2010 with 0.2% one-way friction: 10-month SMA timing had higher terminal value and lower volatility than buy-and-hold, but buy-and-hold led until about 2001, and during the 2000s even random monthly timing tended to beat buy-and-hold, weakening the evidence for the rule itself. Data ends 2010. — [CXO Advisory, 10-month SMA Timing Signals Over the Short Run](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/10-month-sma-timing-signals-over-the-short-run/)
- Multi-asset time-series momentum (not equity-only; futures, long-short, 10% vol target): AQR's 1880-2016 study across 67 markets using 1-, 3- and 12-month lookbacks: 18.0% gross, 11.0% net of costs and 2/20 fees, net Sharpe 0.76, positive in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns. This is the core evidence for trend following and it does not come from long-only single stocks. — [Swedroe (etf.com)](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist)

Antonacci dual momentum (GEM)
- Rule: 12-month lookback for both relative and absolute momentum; rotate between US and foreign stocks, move to bonds when US stocks' trend is negative. ReSolve tested 1,226 specifications (lookbacks 1-18 months and variants) over 1950-2018: the original specification is not statistically special ("all specifications have equal merit"), outperformed in only 61% of bootstrap paths, regressed to the median out of sample (1950-73, 2012-18), and the gap in 5-year cumulative return between the luckiest and unluckiest specification averages 64 percentage points. An ensemble of lookbacks had shallower worst drawdowns (13.2% vs 17.4% median). Data ends 2018. — [ReSolve thread on Global Equity Momentum](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1110908075333038081.html)

Clenow "Stocks on the Move" (rules as quoted from the book)
- Trade only on Wednesdays. Rank S&P 500 stocks by annualized 90-day exponential regression slope multiplied by R-squared. Disqualify stocks below their 100-day moving average or with a gap above 15% in the window. Position size: shares = account value x 0.001 / ATR(20), i.e. each position targets a 10 bp daily move. Buy from the top of the list until cash runs out, only if the S&P 500 is above its 200-day moving average. Weekly: sell anything that leaves the top 20% of the ranking, falls below its 100-day MA, gaps over 15%, or leaves the index. Every second Wednesday: resize positions. — [QuantConnect forum, Clenow momentum implementation](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/10493/andreas-clenow-momentum-strategy-using-framework-from-stocks-on-the-move-book/)
- An independent implementation describes the outcome as: typically 10-30 stocks, minimum account about $30,000, volatility well below the index, 2008 handled well, limited upside with meaningful downside reduction, and returns "roughly on par with the S&P 500 in taxed accounts". No numeric table was retrievable. — [TuringTrader, Clenow's Stocks on the Move](https://turingtrader-com.web.app/portfolios/clenow-stocks-on-the-move)

Breakout / all-time-high trend following on single stocks
- Wilcox and Crittenden (2005): long-only; buy when the close is at or above the all-time highest close; exit on a 10-ATR trailing stop; equal weight; minimum price and liquidity filters. Reported 1983-2004: 19.3%/yr, 15.6% volatility, Sharpe 1.24, max drawdown -33.7%; trade expectancy tested after 0.5% round-trip cost; results insensitive to ATR multiples of 8-12. — [Quantpedia, Trend-Following Effect in Stocks](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/trend-following-effect-in-stocks)
- Zarattini, Pagani and Wilcox (Jan 2025; data 1950-Nov 2024, more than 66,000 trades, out-of-sample 2005-2024): entry at next open after an all-time-high close; exit at next open after a close below a ratcheting stop based on the all-time high and 42-day ATR; universe price above $10 and 42-day average dollar volume above $1M. Fewer than 7% of trades generate the majority of cumulative profits. Portfolio test on Russell 3000 stocks, Jan 1991-Oct 2024: gross CAGR about 15.0-15.2% vs 11.2% for the cap-weighted market, alpha about 6.2%/yr, max drawdown 31.75%. — [CXO Advisory, All-time High Trend Following](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/all-time-high-trend-following); [Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.com/does-trend-following-still-work-on-stocks/); [Harbourfront Quant summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/does-trend-following-still-work-on)
- Important caveats on that result: positions are sized to a 30% annualized portfolio volatility target with leverage up to 200% and weights recomputed daily; net CAGR "depends on portfolio size"; the authors say the base strategy is hard to run below $1M because turnover costs erode returns, and they add a turnover-control algorithm that they say restores viability (no figures on the pages fetched). — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/all-time-high-trend-following); [Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.com/does-trend-following-still-work-on-stocks/)
- The follow-up reports the trade statistics still hold for 2005-2024 with "a modest decline in average trade profitability" after the original paper. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/trend-following-effect-in-stocks)

### Inferences
- Since 2009 an index trend filter has cost return in exchange for lower drawdown: it is insurance, not alpha. For a long-only account it is defensible as a risk overlay (see section 5), not as a way to beat SPY. Whipsaw is the cost: dozens of false exits per real bear market on daily signals; monthly evaluation reduces but does not remove them.
- The all-time-high system's 15% gross vs 11.2% is not comparable with an unlevered 5-20 stock account: it uses up to 2x leverage, a 30% volatility target and hundreds of concurrent positions. With 5-20 positions the outcome depends on catching the under-7% of trades that carry the profit, so dispersion across runs will be very large. Any Kaibot test should report the distribution over random subsets of signals, not one equity curve.
- Clenow's system is cross-sectional momentum plus a stock trend filter plus an index regime filter plus inverse-volatility sizing; the available independent evidence is "lower volatility, about index return". That matches the pattern in section 1.
- GEM-style single-parameter rotation is fragile to the lookback chosen; if used, average several lookbacks.
- O'Neil/Minervini-style discretionary breakout rules (base patterns, volume surges, relative-strength ratings) have no peer-reviewed test that I could find; the closest evidence is the all-time-high and 52-week-high literature above.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evidence found on Minervini/O'Neil rule sets or on Donchian channel breakouts applied to single US stocks long-only.
- No numeric CAGR/Sharpe/drawdown for Clenow's rules over 2016-2026, and none for GEM after 2018, was retrievable (pages paywalled, image-only or blocked).
- Net-of-cost results by account size for Zarattini-Pagani-Wilcox are in the full paper (SSRN 5084316), which was blocked in this session.
- Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012) was not fetched; I found no study of time-series momentum on single stocks, long-only, net of costs.
- No source gave exact CAGR and drawdown of a 10-month SMA on SPY for 2016-2026.

## 3. Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) and earnings-gap continuation

### Takeaway
PEAD was real and survived costs through 2012 on paper (about 0.3%/month net long-short), but multiple recent studies agree it has faded: gone in large caps since about 2006, insignificant outside microcaps over 2001-2024, with only a short window right after the announcement still showing drift. A long-only large-cap PEAD sleeve should be assumed to have roughly zero alpha until Kaibot's own test proves otherwise.

### Cited Findings
- Novy-Marx and Velikov (07/1973-12/2012, long-short deciles, % per month): PEAD on SUE gross 0.72 (t 4.52), turnover 35.1%, costs 0.46, net 0.26 (t 1.60); with 10/20 buy/hold net 0.35 (t 2.26). PEAD on CAR3 (3-day announcement abnormal return) gross 0.91 (t 6.54), turnover 34.7%, costs 0.57, net 0.34 (t 2.41); buy/hold net 0.35 (t 2.58). — [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)
- Martineau, "Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift" (Critical Finance Review): prices now fully reflect earnings surprises on the announcement date; "For large stocks, PEAD have been non-existent since 2006"; it disappeared from small/microcaps only recently. — [Critical Finance Review abstract](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0122)
- UCLA Anderson's account of the same paper: drift faded from non-microcap stocks starting 2001 and was gone by 2006, attributed to decimalization and faster electronic trading. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)
- Subrahmanyam (2025), US stocks Feb 2001-Dec 2024, earnings-drift factor based on announcement-window abnormal returns: t = 2.18 with all stocks, but t = 1.43 (not significant) once microcaps (below the 20th NYSE size percentile, about 3% of market value) are excluded. Recent papers that still find strong drift (e.g. t about 14 in Hirshleifer, Peng and Wang 2025) did not filter microcaps. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)
- Kettell, McInnis and Zhao (2022; 1974-2020, 526,828 firm-quarters): the long-short PEAD return trends down about 2 bp per quarter; its five-year moving average is about 5% in the 1980s-90s, about 4% in the 2000s-early 2010s, 3% or lower in the late 2010s (return measured over the post-announcement window up to the next announcement), and the introduction says it is indistinguishable from zero after 2017 (the paper's figure and introduction are not fully consistent on this). The decline is significant for non-microcaps (t -3.57) and insignificant for microcaps (t -1.42). — [Kettell, McInnis, Zhao, Why Has PEAD Declined Over Time?](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf)
- Same paper, horizon detail: drift in the immediate window, trading days +2 to +6 after the announcement, has strengthened over time (t 3.92); the decline is concentrated in the later window from day +7 to the next earnings date (t -4.68). They attribute the decline mainly to lower persistence of earnings surprises, not only to arbitrage. — [Kettell, McInnis, Zhao](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf)
- Construction that needs no analyst data: SUE = (EPS this quarter - EPS four quarters ago), optionally with drift, divided by the standard deviation of that change (eight prior quarters in the QuantConnect version). EAR = stock's abnormal return over the three-day window centred on the announcement. Brandt et al. rule: quintile sorts on both, long the top-SUE/top-EAR intersection, enter on the second day after the announcement, hold about 60 trading days. — [Quantpedia, Post-Earnings Announcement Effect](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/post-earnings-announcement-effect/); [QuantConnect forum, SUE backtest](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/20418/151-1-standardized-unexpected-earnings-sue-from-151-trading-strategies-to-18-year-backtest-reality/)
- Brandt et al. results (1987-2004, long-short, gross): EAR strategy about 7.55%/yr abnormal return; combined EAR+SUE about 12.5%/yr; restricting to the 1,000 largest firms reduces but does not eliminate returns; EAR returns do not reverse after three quarters; most of the return comes from the long side. Data ends 2004, before the documented disappearance. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/post-earnings-announcement-effect/)
- A hobbyist long-only test (top 5% SUE of a 50-stock universe, monthly rebalance, QuantConnect): 13.62% CAGR over 2007-2026 with Sharpe 0.44 and max drawdown 63.9%; 23.58% CAGR over 2021-2026 with Sharpe 0.64 and max drawdown 28.9%. No benchmark comparison; flagged by the platform as "Likely Overfitting". — [QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/20418/151-1-standardized-unexpected-earnings-sue-from-151-trading-strategies-to-18-year-backtest-reality/)
- Abnormal earnings-announcement returns survive as an anomaly in Hou-Xue-Zhang's replication (long-short, value-weighted; the summary does not state the sample end). — [Alpha Architect, Replicating Anomalies summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/)

### Inferences
- The evidence points the same way from three independent directions (Martineau; Subrahmanyam; Kettell et al.): in liquid large and mid caps, classic 60-day PEAD is no longer reliably there. The QuantConnect long-only result (13.6% CAGR, Sharpe 0.44, 64% drawdown over 2007-2026) is consistent with "market beta plus noise", not alpha.
- If Kaibot tests PEAD at all, the only version with recent support is short and immediate: enter at the close of day +1 or +2, hold about one week (days +2 to +6), ranking by the announcement-window price reaction (EAR/gap) rather than accounting SUE. Price-gap surprise is computable from daily bars plus an earnings calendar (Finnhub), so it fits the free-data constraint.
- Because the surviving drift is concentrated in microcaps (about 3% of market value), a $5k-$100k account could technically trade them, but spreads there are far above 6 bp and the task constraint is liquid large/mid caps.

### Gaps
- Martineau's full tables (drift size by size group and period, surprise definitions) were not accessible; only abstract-level statements are cited.
- No peer-reviewed long-only, large-cap, net-of-cost PEAD or earnings-gap-continuation result for 2016-2026 was found.
- No rigorous evidence found specifically on "earnings gap-up continuation" swing rules (practitioner pages only, not fetched as reliable).

## 4. Short-term reversal (weekly to monthly) and pullback-in-uptrend / oversold systems (RSI-2, IBS)

### Takeaway
Plain weekly/monthly reversal has a small gross premium and is the anomaly most destroyed by costs in academic tests; refined versions (industry-relative, low-volatility names, composite short-term signals) survive only with careful turnover control. Connors-style RSI-2 and IBS rules have only blog-grade or in-sample evidence; reported results are attractive but not independently validated and the IBS edge disappears if you cannot trade at the close.

### Cited Findings
- Novy-Marx and Velikov (07/1963-12/2012, long-short, % per month): short-run (one-month) reversal gross 0.37 (t 1.71), turnover 90.9%/month, costs 1.65, net -1.28 (t -6.02). Industry-relative reversal gross 0.98 (t 5.72), costs 1.78, net -0.80. Industry-relative reversal restricted to low-volatility stocks: gross 1.25 (t 9.36), costs 1.06, net 0.19 (t 1.41), rising to net 0.28 (t 2.41) with a 10/50 buy/hold rule. Without mitigation, no strategy with turnover above 50%/month has a significant positive net return. — [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)
- de Groot, Huij and Zhou: weekly reversal restricted to the 100 largest US stocks (long the 10 worst prior-week performers, short the 10 best; long-short) earns a net 0.29% per week in their Table 7 using broker (Nomura) cost estimates, and remained significant in the most recent decade of their sample. — [Quantpedia, Short-Term Reversal in Stocks](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)
- Blitz, Hanauer, Honarvar, Huisman and van Vliet (FAJ 2023): a composite of five short-term signals (industry-relative reversal, 1-month industry momentum, analyst revisions, seasonality, idiosyncratic volatility) earns about 12%/yr alpha gross vs about 6% for single signals; at 25 bp per trade single signals net about -2%; a rule of buying in the top 10 and holding until the stock drops out of the top 50 (percentiles, as I read the summary) lifts the composite's net alpha above 6%; alpha stays significant up to about 55 bp per trade; post-publication decay 18-40% but still significant; the authors say results carry over to long-only. — [Alpha Architect, Alpha from Short-Term Signals](https://alphaarchitect.com/alpha-from-short-term-signals-may-survive-market-frictions/)
- Connors RSI-2 rule set as tested by one blogger on Nasdaq 100 constituents, Dec 2006 to late 2025, 4 position slots: close above 200-day MA; close below 5-day MA; RSI(2) below 10; buy next open; exit next open after a close above the 5-day MA; if several signals, take the lowest RSI(2). Reported: 17.84% annual return, win rate 64.3%, average winner +2.33%, average loser -3.02%, profit factor 1.45, max drawdown 29.15%, Sharpe 1.10; the author says it "kept pace with the market". No cost assumptions, benchmark numbers or constituent-history method stated. — [Backtest substack, The 2-Period RSI](https://backtest.substack.com/p/the-2-period-rsi-a-simple-system)
- CXO Advisory's review of Connors' book: the supporting tests (1995-2007) exclude transaction costs, do not correct for data-mining bias and lack out-of-sample or subperiod testing, so they likely overstate future performance. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=2679)
- IBS = (Close - Low) / (High - Low). Tested on 15 country ETFs, 2009-2019, daily: long lowest-IBS / short highest-IBS, enter at the close, exit next close: pre-cost Sharpe 2.9-3.7 (long-short). With open-to-open execution instead, Sharpe falls to between -0.27 and 0.36. Performance decays as the holding period extends beyond one day. All in-sample; commissions not modelled. — [Using Internal Bar Strength as a Key Indicator for Trading Country ETFs (arXiv 2306.12434)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12434)
- In Hou-Xue-Zhang's replication, 93% of liquidity/trading-friction variables (95 of 102) fail to replicate; Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen get 63% for the same group using CAPM alphas vs 4% in Hou et al. — [Alpha Architect, Replicating Anomalies summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/); [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (NBER w28432)](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cost arithmetic for Kaibot: a weekly-turnover book does about 52 round trips a year, about 3.1%/yr at 6 bp. The unconditional one-month reversal premium is only about 4.4%/yr gross long-short (t 1.71), so the unconditional version has no margin even at Kaibot's low cost; this is consistent with Kaibot's own zero-edge intraday dip result.
- What the literature supports is conditional reversal: within-industry, in low-volatility large caps, in stocks above their long-term trend, held a few days, with hysteresis. Kaibot's cost (6 bp) is well under the 25-55 bp per trade thresholds in Blitz et al., so the composite short-term approach is the most defensible mean-reversion candidate, but the analyst-revision input is not in the free-data set and would need replacing or dropping.
- RSI-2 with a 200-day filter is structurally "buy 5-day losers among long-term winners". The 17.8%/Sharpe 1.10 figure is unaudited (possible survivorship in constituents, no costs). The reported average loser being larger than the average winner means the edge depends entirely on win rate; a regime where dips keep falling (2008, March 2020, 2022) is the failure mode, and there are no stops in the canonical rules.
- IBS needs market-on-close execution on the signal day; a system that decides after the close and trades next open loses the effect.

### Gaps
- No independent, cost-inclusive, out-of-sample study of RSI-2 or IBS on US large caps for 2016-2026 was found; no evidence quantifying decay since Connors' 2008 publication.
- de Groot-Huij-Zhou's sample end date and annualized figure were not on the page fetched.
- Long-only alpha figures and the sample period for Blitz et al. were not on the summary page.

## 5. Defensive overlays: volatility management, index trend filters, low-volatility and quality tilts

### Takeaway
None of the overlays has reliably raised risk-adjusted return for an unlevered long-only investor out of sample; they reduce drawdown and volatility at a cost in return. Volatility-managed portfolios look strong in-sample but do not hold up in real time; low-volatility has lagged the S&P 500 by about 5 points a year over the last decade and quality by about 1 point.

### Cited Findings
- Moreira and Muir construction: next month's exposure = c / (previous month's realized variance from daily returns), no estimation needed. Market factor 1926-2015: alpha 4.86%/yr (t about 3.1), appraisal ratio about 0.33, Sharpe up about 25%; momentum alpha 12.51%/yr. — [Moreira and Muir, Volatility Managed Portfolios (NBER w22208)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208.pdf)
- Leverage matters: baseline market weights have median 0.93, 90th percentile 2.64, 99th percentile 6.39. Capping exposure at 1.0 (no leverage) leaves alpha of 2.12%/yr; capping at 1.5 gives 3.10%. Alpha after 10 bp costs 3.98%; break-even cost about 56 bp. The strategy was almost fully out after the fall 2008 declines. — [Moreira and Muir](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208.pdf)
- Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan (JFE 2020), 103 equity strategies: in direct comparison the volatility-managed version has a higher Sharpe in 53 cases and lower in 50; only 8 are significantly better. In-sample spanning alphas are positive for 77 of 103, but the implied strategies cannot be implemented in real time; the real-time version beats the original on Sharpe in only 45 of 103 cases. For the market factor, real-time Sharpe is 0.42 vs 0.46 unmanaged. Cause: unstable scaling coefficients. — [Cederburg et al. paper](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/)
- Index trend filter: see section 2. Out-of-sample 2006-2016 the 10-month SMA on US stocks had half the volatility (7.4% vs 14.7%) and a 16.7% vs 51% max drawdown with a slightly higher return, but lagged in six of the eight years after 2009; the 200-day rule has underperformed buy-and-hold in every period since the financial crisis through 2024. — [Swedroe (etf.com)](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist); [Of Dollars and Data](https://ofdollarsanddata.com/why-trend-following-is-harder-than-it-looks/)
- Live factor ETFs, annualized, as of an unstated 2026 date: USMV (minimum volatility) 10-year 10.54%, 5-year 7.72%; QUAL (quality) 10-year 14.61%, 5-year 11.71%; MTUM 10-year 15.79%, 5-year 11.03%. — [StockAnalysis multi-ETF comparison](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/rsp-vs-iwm-vs-vtv-vs-schd-vs-mtum-vs-usmv-vs-vug-vs-qual/)
- S&P 500 reference from a sister page (as-of date also unstated, so only approximately comparable): VOO 10-year 15.46%, 5-year 13.26%. — [StockAnalysis MTUM vs VOO](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/mtum-vs-voo/)
- Low risk and quality are among the 13 factor themes in Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen; ten of 13 themes replicate above 75% (the exceptions are seasonality, leverage and size). These are long-short, risk-adjusted results. — [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (NBER w28432)](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)

### Inferences
- For an unlevered long-only account, volatility targeting can only scale down. The in-sample value of that (2.12%/yr alpha at a 1.0 cap) shrinks to roughly nothing in real time per Cederburg et al. Treat it as drawdown control: expect lower volatility and smaller crashes, similar-or-lower Sharpe, lower CAGR in bull markets.
- The one place volatility scaling has out-of-sample support is momentum itself (section 1). The practical long-only form is: cut the momentum sleeve's gross exposure when its recent realized volatility is high and/or when the index is below its long moving average. That is exactly what Clenow's index filter plus ATR sizing does.
- Low-volatility's alpha in the academic record is risk-adjusted (CAPM alpha with beta well under 1). In a decade when the index compounded about 15%/yr, a beta-0.7 portfolio necessarily lags on raw return, which is what USMV shows. It should not be sold to the owner as a way to beat SPY.
- Quality as a screen (profitable, low leverage) is cheap, low-turnover and close to index-like in return; reasonable as a universe filter for momentum, not as a return source.

### Gaps
- Barroso and Detzel (transaction-cost critique of volatility-managed portfolios) and the Man Group volatility-targeting study were not fetched.
- No Sharpe, volatility or max-drawdown figures for USMV/QUAL over 2016-2026 on the pages fetched, so the "better risk-adjusted" claim for low-vol in the last decade is neither confirmed nor refuted here.
- Robeco's own low-volatility updates were not fetched.

## 6. Which results are robust out of sample (replication studies)

### Takeaway
Momentum-family anomalies are the most robust price-based results in every major replication, but effect sizes are consistently smaller than first reported (about one third lower out of sample, more than half lower after publication), and trading-friction/short-horizon signals are the least robust.

### Cited Findings
- McLean and Pontiff (2016): anomaly returns are 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower post-publication. — [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (NBER w28432)](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)
- Hou, Xue and Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies": with NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted returns, 286 of 447 anomalies (64%) are insignificant at |t| < 1.96 and 380 (85%) at a t-cutoff of 3; 95 of 102 liquidity variables fail; the 161 survivors have much smaller magnitudes than originally reported. Survivors include 12-1 momentum, industry momentum, residual momentum, customer momentum and abnormal earnings-announcement returns. Microcaps are about 60% of stocks but about 3% of market value, and drive many original results. (This summary describes the working-paper counts.) — [Alpha Architect, Replicating Anomalies](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/)
- Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen: 153 factors, 93 countries, 13 themes. US replication rate 84.0% (Bayesian, CAPM-alpha based) vs the 35% they attribute to Hou et al. (18% after multiple-testing correction); with OLS raw returns their own US rate is 56.9%. By size: mega caps 77.3%, large caps 81.5%. — [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (NBER w28432)](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)
- Same paper on decay: average in-sample alpha 0.45%/month vs 0.31% out of sample (about one third lower); the cross-sectional slope of out-of-sample on in-sample alpha is only 0.25 post-original-sample. — [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (NBER w28432)](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)
- The difference between "35% replicate" and "84% replicate" is largely definitional: raw long-short returns vs CAPM alphas, and how multiple testing is handled; both agree effects shrink. — [Jensen, Kelly, Pedersen (NBER w28432)](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)
- For momentum specifically: 10.9%/yr pre-publication vs 5.5%/yr in 1994-2015. — [Swedroe (etf.com)](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-really-dead)
- Novy-Marx and Velikov's cost screen: most anomalies with monthly turnover below 50% keep significant net spreads when traded cost-consciously; few above 50% do. — [Alpha Architect, transaction costs](https://alphaarchitect.com/transaction-costs/); [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ranking by robustness for Kaibot's purposes: (1) 12-1 momentum and close relatives (residual, industry) replicate everywhere, shrink about half after publication; (2) earnings-announcement return replicated through the mid-2010s but section 3 shows it has since faded outside microcaps; (3) low-risk and quality replicate as risk-adjusted alphas, not raw outperformance; (4) short-horizon reversal and trading-friction signals are the weakest and most cost-sensitive.
- All replication studies test long-short portfolios, mostly gross of costs, and "replicates" means "statistically non-zero", not "beats SPY long-only". A sensible prior is to haircut any published long-short premium by half for publication decay and by half again for long-only, before costs.

### Gaps
- Theme-level replication rates for momentum, low risk and quality in Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen are in figures that did not render as text.
- Hou-Xue-Zhang's published-version counts (commonly cited as 452 anomalies, 65% failing) were not verified against the journal article.

## 7. Realistic expectations for a long-only, unlevered, 5-20 position large/mid-cap portfolio versus SPY

### Takeaway
On the published evidence, none of these families can honestly be expected to beat SPY by a wide margin net of costs in liquid large caps; the realistic range is roughly SPY minus 2 to SPY plus 2 points a year, with the main controllable difference being the drawdown/volatility profile. Momentum with a trend filter is the best-supported core; PEAD and plain reversal should be treated as unproven until Kaibot's own out-of-sample tests say otherwise.

### Cited Findings (benchmarks the estimates rest on)
- S&P 500 index fund (VOO): 10-year annualized 15.46%, 5-year 13.26% (as-of date unstated, 2026). — [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/mtum-vs-voo/)
- Long-only large-cap momentum, live: MTUM 10-year 15.79-16.50% annualized depending on as-of date; 5-year between 11.03% and 13.01% on those pages (behind VOO), but 104% vs 89% cumulative over the 5 years to 16 June 2026 after a 30% first-half 2026. — [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/mtum-vs-voo/); [StockAnalysis multi-ETF](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/compare/rsp-vs-iwm-vs-vtv-vs-schd-vs-mtum-vs-usmv-vs-vug-vs-qual/); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/06/17/forget-the-plain-sp-500-the-momentum-version-captured-the-run-and-beat-it-by-15-this-year/)
- SPMO 5-year annualized 19.7% vs 12.8% to 31 Aug 2026, with about 54% in technology. — [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/18/spmo-owns-the-sp-500s-100-fastest-rising-stocks-its-momentum-screen-has-beaten-the-index-by-67-points-over-the-last-five-years/)
- Momentum mutual funds historically captured none of the paper premium (value-add about -2.2%/yr). — [Research Affiliates via etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-can-momentum-investing-be-saved)

- The single most important caveat for the owner: over 2016-2026 the S&P 500 itself compounded about 15%/yr, led by a handful of mega-cap growth stocks.
